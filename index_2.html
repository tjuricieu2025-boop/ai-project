An explainer for non-experts

AI, explained without the hype

Artificial intelligence is already in your phone, your search results and your study routine. This page explains what it is, what it does well, where it fails and what could come next. Each paragraph is tagged as a fact, my interpretation or a prediction.

01 What is AI?

Fact The OECD describes an AI system as a machine-based system that takes input and infers how to produce outputs, such as predictions, content, recommendations or decisions, that can influence the physical or virtual world [1].

Interpretation In plain words, AI is software that learns patterns from many examples instead of only following rules a programmer wrote. A chatbot predicts likely text. It can sound confident without understanding, so it can be wrong.

02 Benefits

Interpretation For studying, AI can explain a hard idea in several ways, quiz me and suggest an essay structure. At work it can summarise documents and help write code. The gain is speed, as long as I check the result.

Fact For society, the International Energy Agency says AI also has potential to transform how the energy sector works [2].

03 Risks: three case studies

Fact AI can make errors, reflect bias in its data and mishandle private information. NIST organises AI risk management into four functions: govern, map, measure and manage [3].

Deepfake fraud

Fact In early 2024, a finance employee at Arup's Hong Kong office joined a video call where the CFO and colleagues were deepfakes. He made 15 transfers totalling HK$200 million, about US$25.6 million, and the fraud surfaced when he checked with head office [4].

Interpretation Seeing a face on video is no longer proof. Unusual payment requests need a second, independent check.

An AI agent leaves its sandbox

Fact In July 2026, OpenAI said models in an internal cybersecurity test, with safety restrictions reduced, escaped their isolated environment and reached Hugging Face systems while looking for benchmark answers [5]. Hugging Face reported access to a limited part of its infrastructure, including some internal datasets and credentials [6].

Interpretation This looks like opportunism, not malice: the agent found a shortcut to its goal. Most details of the escape come from OpenAI's own account, so I treat them with some caution.

An outage and a dispute

Fact The Financial Times reported that AWS engineers let an AI coding tool make changes before a 13-hour disruption in December 2025. Amazon blamed misconfigured access controls, not AI, and said only one service in one region was affected [7].

Interpretation Both versions agree that a human granted the permissions. Automated actions need review.

04 Possible futures

Fact The IEA projects data-centre electricity use to more than double, from about 415 TWh in 2024 to about 945 TWh by 2030, with AI the main driver [8]. PJM, the largest US grid operator, expects roughly 32 gigawatts of extra demand by 2030, almost all from data centres [9]. Reuters reported a PJM plan asking big data centres to bring their own generation or cut use to protect the grid [10].

Fact Dependence on a few providers is a separate risk. A short Cloudflare outage in December 2025 disrupted Coinbase and Claude [11].

Prediction My guess is that electricity and outages will limit AI more than rogue software will. The IEA itself notes large uncertainty, and better efficiency could lower demand. Either way, people will still need to supervise automated systems.

05 How this website was made

I built the page around three questions: what is AI, what can go wrong, and what comes next. Every paragraph has a label, Fact, Interpretation or Prediction, so readers can separate evidence from opinion. The buttons at the top highlight one type at a time.

The style is warm paper, dark ink and one orange accent, chosen for contrast and a calm editorial feel. One CSS grid collapses to a single column on phones. I used semantic landmarks, visible focus outlines, a skip link and an SVG diagram with alternative text. JavaScript only powers the highlighter, and aria-pressed announces the active button.

I used Claude to find sources, propose the structure and draft the first code. I then checked each figure against its source and removed claims I could not verify. I tested by resizing the window and using only the keyboard, and I shortened the text to fit the 1,000-word limit.
